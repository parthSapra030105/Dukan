# Lore

The narrative thread. Use this for: pitch openings, interview talking points, LinkedIn posts, README hook, the first 30 seconds of any video.

## The origin

In Pune, a friend was preparing a pitch to a 6-outlet local supermarket chain called RK Bazar. The thesis was simple: their biggest hidden cost is the army of phone operators they hire to take orders, and the second hidden cost is the calls they don't even pick up during evening rush.

I went to the meeting in spirit, not in person. But the problem stuck.

The store owner ran the numbers in his head out loud. Four people per outlet on phones. ₹80k per outlet per month in salaries. Six outlets. Half a million rupees just to answer the phone. And evenings, the lines stayed busy and customers gave up — going to Zepto or Blinkit instead.

He wasn't asking for AI. He was asking for breathing room.

## The framing shift

The wrong frame for this problem is "build a chatbot." Chatbots fail because customers want to *talk*, not type. The right frame is "build an operator who doesn't sleep and knows the whole catalog by heart." That's a voice AI agent — but only if it can handle Hindi mixed with English, recognise his ten thousand regulars, and route the hard cases to a human in under two seconds.

That kind of voice AI exists now. It didn't a year ago.

## Why Indian retail, not Indian BFSI

Most Indian voice-AI vendors aim at BFSI because the cheques are bigger. But banks have already invested in call centres; they're optimising. Local supermarkets have never had a voice-AI option — they've been told they're too small to matter.

There are tens of thousands of these stores. The market that's "too small to matter" is actually a market that's never been served properly.

## Why now

Three things changed:
1. Voice quality crossed the uncanny-valley line in 2024 — for Indian English and Hindi specifically
2. Indian telecom is more programmable than it was three years ago — virtual numbers + webhook routing in 30 minutes
3. Margin pressure on local retail from 10-minute delivery apps reached the point of action

## The personal stake

I'm building this for the Bolna assignment, but I'm building it because I want it to exist. The store I'm thinking of is a real store. The owner is a real person. If a version of this product ends up in his shop in 2027, the path back to this moment is straight.

> "The market that's been told it's too small to matter is exactly the one I want to build for."

> "Bolna says they handle dialects, code-switching, escalation. This use case stress-tests all three at once."
